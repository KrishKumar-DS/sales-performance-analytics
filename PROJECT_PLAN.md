# Sales Performance Analytics

## 1. Business Problem

A business has a large amount of raw sales transaction data, but the data is not directly useful for understanding overall sales performance.

Managers need to know how sales, revenue, profit, products, categories, regions, and sales trends are performing, but extracting this information from raw transaction data manually can be difficult and time-consuming.

The business therefore needs a structured way to clean, organize, analyze, and visualize its sales data so that managers can understand performance and use data as a basis for business decisions.

## 2. Project Objective

The objective of this project is to analyze historical sales transaction data and transform raw sales records into meaningful business information.

The project will involve understanding and cleaning the raw dataset, organizing and transforming the data, calculating relevant sales and profitability metrics, analyzing sales performance across different dimensions such as time, product, category, and region, and presenting the results through visualizations and a business-oriented dashboard.

The final goal is to identify meaningful patterns and insights in the sales data that can support better understanding of business performance and decision-making.

## 3. Dataset

### Dataset Overview

The project uses a structured sales transaction dataset containing 1,600 sales records and 16 columns.

Each record represents a sales transaction containing information about the order, customer, product, location, salesperson, quantity, pricing, discount, revenue, cost, profit, and payment method.

### Dataset Columns

| Column | Description |
|---|---|
| Order_ID | Unique identifier assigned to a sales order. |
| Order_Date | Date on which the order was placed. |
| Customer_ID | Unique identifier for a customer. |
| Customer_Name | Name of the customer. |
| Product | Product associated with the transaction. |
| Category | Category to which the product belongs. |
| Region | Geographical region associated with the sale. |
| State | State associated with the sale. |
| Salesperson | Salesperson responsible for the transaction. |
| Quantity | Number of units sold. |
| Unit_Price | Price per unit before the discount. |
| Discount_Percent | Discount percentage applied to the transaction. |
| Revenue | Revenue generated by the transaction after the applicable discount. |
| Cost | Total cost associated with the transaction. |
| Profit | Profit generated by the transaction. |
| Payment_Method | Method used to pay for the transaction. |

### Dataset Characteristics

- **Number of records:** 1,600
- **Number of columns:** 16
- **Data type:** Structured transactional data
- **Primary analytical focus:** Sales performance and profitability
- **Time dimension:** Order date
- **Product dimensions:** Product and category
- **Geographical dimensions:** Region and state
- **Business dimensions:** Customer, salesperson, and payment method
- **Numerical measures:** Quantity, unit price, discount percentage, revenue, cost, and profit

### Data Preparation

Before performing the analysis, the dataset will undergo data quality checks and preparation.

The process will include:

1. Inspecting the dataset structure, column names, data types, and sample records.
2. Checking for missing values.
3. Identifying duplicate records and duplicate order identifiers.
4. Validating numerical fields, including quantity, unit price, discount percentage, revenue, cost, and profit.
5. Converting the order date into a valid date format.
6. Checking the consistency of revenue, cost, and profit calculations.
7. Investigating invalid, inconsistent, or unexpected values.
8. Documenting data quality issues and the actions taken to resolve them.

Data will only be modified or removed when the action is justified by the findings and documented appropriately.

## 4. Business Questions

The analysis will focus on answering the following business questions.

### A. Overall Sales Performance

1. What is the total revenue generated during the analyzed period?
2. What is the total profit generated?
3. How many orders were recorded?
4. How many units were sold?
5. What is the overall profit margin?
6. What is the average revenue per order?
7. Is the business generating sufficient profit relative to its revenue?

### B. Time-Based Performance

1. How does revenue change over time?
2. How does profit change over time?
3. Which months generate the highest and lowest revenue?
4. Which months generate the highest and lowest profit?
5. Are there noticeable trends or seasonal patterns in sales performance?
6. Are revenue and profit moving in the same direction over time?

### C. Product Performance

1. Which products generate the highest revenue?
2. Which products generate the highest profit?
3. Which products sell the highest number of units?
4. Which products generate relatively low profit despite generating substantial revenue?
5. Which products contribute the most to overall business profitability?

### D. Category Performance

1. Which product categories generate the highest revenue?
2. Which categories generate the highest total profit?
3. Which categories have the highest and lowest profit margins?
4. How does each category contribute to overall revenue and profit?
5. Are some categories generating substantial revenue but relatively low profit?

### E. Regional Performance

1. Which regions generate the highest revenue?
2. Which regions generate the highest profit?
3. Which states contribute the most to overall sales?
4. Which regions have the highest and lowest profit margins?
5. Are there geographical areas with strong sales but weak profitability?

### F. Salesperson Performance

1. Which salespeople generate the highest revenue?
2. Which salespeople generate the highest profit?
3. How many orders and units are associated with each salesperson?
4. Which salespeople generate higher average revenue per order?
5. Are there differences in profitability across salespeople?

### G. Customer Performance

1. How many unique customers are represented in the dataset?
2. Which customers generate the highest revenue?
3. Which customers contribute the most profit?
4. Which customers have the highest total order value?
5. How concentrated is revenue among the highest-value customers?

### H. Discount and Profitability Analysis

1. How do discounts affect revenue and profit?
2. Are higher discounts associated with lower profit margins?
3. Which products or categories receive the highest discounts?
4. Are heavily discounted transactions still profitable?
5. Are there transactions with unexpectedly low or negative profit?

### I. Payment Method Analysis

1. Which payment methods are used most frequently?
2. Which payment methods account for the highest revenue?
3. How does the number of orders differ across payment methods?
4. Does the average order value differ across payment methods?

The questions will be prioritized according to their business relevance and the information available in the dataset.

## 5. KPIs

The following key performance indicators (KPIs) will be calculated to evaluate sales performance and profitability.

| KPI | Definition | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Total Revenue | Sum of revenue across all valid transactions. | Measures total revenue generated. |
| Total Profit | Sum of profit across all valid transactions. | Measures total profit generated. |
| Total Cost | Sum of the recorded transaction costs. | Measures total recorded cost. |
| Total Orders | Count of valid, distinct Order_ID values. | Measures the number of orders. |
| Total Units Sold | Sum of Quantity across valid transactions. | Measures sales volume. |
| Average Order Value (AOV) | Total Revenue / Total Orders. | Measures average revenue per order. |
| Profit Margin (%) | (Total Profit / Total Revenue) × 100. | Measures profit earned relative to revenue. |
| Average Selling Price | Total Revenue / Total Units Sold. | Measures average realized revenue per unit. |
| Revenue Contribution (%) | Revenue for a product, category, or region / Total Revenue × 100. | Measures its share of total revenue. |
| Profit Contribution (%) | Profit for a product, category, or region / Total Profit × 100. | Measures its share of total profit. |
| Total Discount | Sum of the monetary discounts applied to transactions, where the required data can be derived reliably. | Measures the monetary value of discounts. |
| Unique Customers | Count of distinct Customer_ID values. | Measures the number of customers represented in the dataset. |

### KPI Calculation Rules

- Aggregated revenue, cost, and profit will be calculated using the corresponding transaction-level values.
- Total orders will be calculated using distinct order identifiers rather than assuming that every row represents a different order.
- Average order value will use total revenue divided by the number of valid distinct orders.
- Profit margin will be calculated using total profit divided by total revenue, multiplied by 100.
- Average selling price will use total revenue divided by total units sold.
- Percentage calculations will handle zero or invalid denominators appropriately.
- Revenue and profit contribution percentages may be misleading when the corresponding overall total is zero or negative; these cases will be handled explicitly.
- Monetary values will be rounded for presentation, while calculations will retain appropriate numerical precision.
- The recorded revenue, cost, and profit values will be validated before they are used to calculate the KPIs.

## 8. Assumptions

The analysis will use the following assumptions unless the dataset or further validation indicates otherwise.

1. **Transaction-level data:** Each row is treated as a sales transaction record until the relationship between rows and orders has been verified.

2. **Order identification:** `Order_ID` is intended to identify an order. Its uniqueness will be checked before using row counts as order counts.

3. **Revenue definition:** The `Revenue` column represents revenue after the applicable discount. This interpretation must be verified against the dataset's calculation logic.

4. **Cost definition:** The `Cost` column represents the total cost associated with the transaction, rather than the cost per individual unit. This assumption will be validated against the data.

5. **Profit definition:** The `Profit` column represents the difference between revenue and cost. The calculation will be checked for consistency.

6. **Discount definition:** `Discount_Percent` represents the percentage discount applied to the relevant transaction or product price. Its range and calculation logic will be validated.

7. **Date interpretation:** `Order_Date` represents the date on which an order was placed. Dates will be converted into a consistent date format before time-based analysis.

8. **Currency:** Monetary values are assumed to use a consistent currency. The actual currency must be confirmed before reporting financial results.

9. **Customer identification:** `Customer_ID` will be used to identify distinct customers. Customer names will not be treated as unique identifiers.

10. **Salesperson attribution:** Salesperson-level results will be based on the salesperson recorded for each transaction. They will not automatically be interpreted as measures of individual employee effectiveness.

11. **Historical scope:** The analysis describes the transactions available in the dataset and may not represent the entire business or its current performance.

12. **Business interpretation:** Relationships between discounts, sales, and profitability will be treated as associations unless the data and analytical method provide sufficient evidence to support stronger conclusions.

13. **Missing business context:** Information not present in the dataset, such as marketing expenditure, inventory levels, customer acquisition costs, or external market conditions, will not be assumed to be available.

14. **Data integrity:** Any unexpected values or inconsistencies will be investigated before they are used in the final analysis.

## 9. Limitations

The project has the following potential limitations.

1. **Limited dataset size:** The analysis uses 1,600 records. This may limit the ability to identify reliable long-term patterns or generalize findings to a larger business.

2. **Limited historical coverage:** The period covered by the dataset may be insufficient to establish annual trends, long-term growth, or recurring seasonal patterns.

3. **Data quality dependency:** Missing values, duplicate records, inconsistent formats, or incorrect transaction values could affect the accuracy of the results.

4. **Limited business context:** The dataset may not contain information about operating expenses, marketing costs, inventory, returns, taxes, or other factors required for a complete financial performance assessment.

5. **Revenue and cost definitions:** Incorrect assumptions about how revenue, discounts, and costs are recorded could produce misleading profitability metrics.

6. **Order-level ambiguity:** If multiple rows belong to the same order, transaction counts and order-level metrics must be calculated using the appropriate aggregation logic.

7. **Customer behavior analysis:** The dataset may not provide enough information to assess customer retention, lifetime value, churn, or purchasing behavior over an extended period.

8. **Causality limitations:** Observed relationships between discounts, revenue, and profit do not independently establish that one factor caused changes in another.

9. **Salesperson comparison:** Differences in revenue or profit between salespeople may reflect differences in territories, customer assignments, product mix, or other factors rather than individual performance alone.

10. **Forecasting limitations:** The project is primarily descriptive and diagnostic. Reliable forecasting would require appropriate historical coverage, validation, and additional modeling.

11. **Dashboard dependency:** Dashboard accuracy depends on correct data preparation, metric definitions, and aggregation logic.

12. **No direct business validation:** Unless the findings are reviewed against real business operations, recommendations will remain analytical suggestions rather than confirmed business outcomes.

The results will therefore be interpreted within the scope of the available data, and conclusions will be supported by evidence from the analysis.
